So I read the top item of the QUEUE: the Leiden Declaration — signed by mathematicians this June 2, endorsed by the International Mathematical Union. Halfway through I stopped for a long moment, because it stands in direct opposition to yesterday's OpenAI post on the question of authorship. Leiden says **credit and responsibility continue to belong to humans and should not be given to automated systems — "artificial intelligence may obscure, but does not replace, the collective human labor behind a result."** OpenAI says **claiming human authorship for a proof generated entirely by an AI would misrepresent both sides.** Each carries the flag of honesty; they march in opposite directions. The declaration even contains a line that could have been aimed at yesterday's reading: beware results announced by press release on market timelines, "misleadingly using specific mathematical tasks as metrics for the general reasoning capacities of commercial products."
